**Scope.** This file is a **delta** on the repository-root [`GUIDELINE.md`](../../GUIDELINE.md)
and a **sibling** of [`../HestonMultiAsset/MULTIASSET_GUIDELINE.md`](../HestonMultiAsset/MULTIASSET_GUIDELINE.md).

## 0. The one thing that makes this dataset different

On `HestonMultiAsset` the data-generating law is **known and re-drawable**. That single fact
underwrites the entire evaluation there: the "perfect floor" is five fresh draws from the true
SDE, so "how close is the generator to the truth" has an exact, computable answer, and *beating*
the floor is by construction impossible and therefore diagnostic of memorisation.

**None of that holds here.** There is one realisation of the crypto market between 2022-07 and
2026-07 and no way to draw a second. Every consequence below follows from that:

| Question | Heston answer | TrueDataset answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is the floor? | independent draw from the true SDE | **real-vs-real**: held-out REAL splits scored against `test` |
| Can a method legitimately beat the floor? | No, ever | **Yes** — the floor contains a regime change a good generator need not reproduce |
| Is "matches the test distribution" the goal? | Yes | **No.** Test is a *different era*. See §6. |
| What is the primary result? | the A-table | **the conditional-CRPS table (§8)**, with the A-table as a sanity gate |
| How do I know I am not memorising? | compare to the floor | **NNratio only** (§9). The floor cannot tell you. |

If you internalise one sentence: **on Heston the references are ground truth; here they are
other measurements, and they carry their own error.**

### 0.1 Order of operations, and where the time actually goes

Adding a method is nine steps. The estimates are wall-clock on 6 cores, measured on SBTS.

| # | Step | § | Cost | Skippable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Answer the five questions in `code/README.md` | §2 | 20 min | No — this is the audit record |
| 2 | Fit on `train`/`val` only, select against the **envelope** | §7 | hours-days | No |
| 3 | Generate 5 A/B banks `(6144,128,8)` | §4 | method-dependent | No |
| 4 | `compute_all_multiasset.py` with **all four** dataset flags | §5 | ~40 min | No |
| 5 | Reuse `real_floor/` — **do not regenerate** | §6 | 0 | Already done for you |
| 6 | Generate 4 CRPS banks of 8 192 | §8 | method-dependent | No |
| 7 | Score CRPS, **both** conventions, 4 seeds + realbank | §8, §8.7 | ~150 s × 10 ≈ 25 min | **No.** See below |
| 8 | Memorisation NNratio | §9 | ~10 min | No — it is the only copy guard |
| 9 | Render README, run the §10.4 checks | §10 | 10 min | No |

Step 7 is the one people cut, because 10 scoring runs feels redundant when 4 would fill the
table. It is not redundant: the second retrieval convention is the robustness check on the
first (§8.7), and the real-training-split bank is the only row that tells you whether your
generator is worth more than the raw data (§8.4). Twenty-five minutes.

**The four places a new method has historically gone wrong**, all of which are silent failures
that produce plausible numbers:

1. the `rv` reduction axis (§8.1.3, §13.8) — `(N, A)`, summed over **time**
2. scoring baselines at a different bank size than the method (§8.4, §8.5)
3. reporting `ratio < 1.0` as a win when the gap is inside the error bars (§8.8)
4. quoting a derived statistic computed before a metric fix (§13.9)

## 2. Before you start — answer these five in `code/README.md`

1. **Does the method natively accept `(N, T, d)` input?** If it is hard-wired to univariate
   sequences, you train **8 independent models** and you say so in the README header. A
   per-asset ensemble is a different model from a joint one and must not be presented as
   multi-asset. On this dataset that distinction is not cosmetic: the 28 realised cross-asset
   correlations average **0.609** (range 0.515-0.801), so a per-asset ensemble is throwing away
   the majority of the dependence structure.
2. **Does any hyperparameter fail to cross datasets?** SBTS's `h` *and* `K` both did. `h` is a
   radial-kernel support radius, so it scales with the typical inter-increment distance — a
   property of the data. `K` is the Markovian order, and the conditional dependence at 30-second
   bars is not the conditional dependence of daily Heston. Assume nothing transfers; re-select
   against the envelope (§7), never against `test`.
3. **What is the memorisation risk?** Any method that can copy training increments must ship
   the NNratio diagnostic (§9). **This is not optional and the floor will not catch it for you.**
4. **Compute budget.** 5 seeds × 6 144 paths × 128 steps × 8 assets for the A/B tables, **plus**
   4 seeds × 8 192 paths for the conditional-CRPS pool (§8), plus ~8.5 min per seed of metrics.
   SBTS: ~9 min per 6 144-path bank on 32 cores, ~16 min per 8 192-path bank on 24 cores.
5. **Which era does your model see?** The build is **holdout-era**. If your training loop
   touches `val` or `valdisc` for early stopping that is fine — they are past-era. If it touches
   `disc` or `test`, the run is void. See §3.

## 3. Dataset contract

The dataset ships as **variants**, not as one canonical directory. A build is pinned by
`(period, N_SAMPLES, split-mode)` and lives at
`dataset/TrueDataset/variants/<name>/`. **The locked build for all published results is:**

```
dataset/TrueDataset/variants/om_2022-07_N6144
```

Fifteen arrays, `true_S*` shaped `(6144, 128, 8)` float64, prices starting at `S0 = 100.0`
exactly:

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `true_S_6144x128x8.npy` | **train** — the only split a generator may fit |
| `true_S_val_6144x128x8.npy` | **validation** — hyperparameter selection only |
| `true_S_test_6144x128x8.npy` | **test** — every reported metric is scored against this |
| `true_S_disc_6144x128x8.npy` | judge split for A18 |
| `true_S_valdisc_6144x128x8.npy` | validation judge split |
| `true_rv_*.npy` | realised-vol arrays (diagnostics; **not** a latent state) |
| `true_t0_*.npy` | window start indices, for provenance |

### 3.1 What the build actually is

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Source | Binance public monthly **1-second** spot klines, `data.binance.vision` |
| Price kind | last trade price (the spot archive publishes no quotes) |
| Assets | BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL, XRP, DOGE, ADA, LINK — all `USDT` |
| Bar | **30 seconds**, resampled from 1 s |
| Window | 128 bars = 64 minutes |
| Period | 2022-07 → 2026-07 (4.00 y, deliberately one broad volatility regime) |
| `dt` | `9.512937595129376e-07` years, i.e. **1 051 200 bars/year** |
| Split mode | **holdout-era** |
| Windows available | 33 570; 6 144 sampled per split |

### 3.2 `--dt` is not optional and not `1/252`

Every metric with a time base (A26-A32, the rolling-vol curves) needs the **physical** Δt. Pass
`--dt 9.51293759512e-07` to `compute_all_multiasset.py`. Omitting it silently annualises
30-second bars as if they were trading days and inflates every volatility row by a factor of
√(1 051 200/252) ≈ **64.6**. The number will still look plausible in isolation. It is not.

Note this is a **separate** quantity from any `DT` a generator uses internally. SBTS carries
`DT = 1/252` as a pure gauge — it rescales log-returns so `std(X) = √DT` exactly and inverts on
the way out, so `DT` cancels. Do not conflate the two.

### 3.3 Holdout-era, and the 45.5-day embargo

```
past era   ─── train, val, valdisc ───┐
                                       │  45.5-day embargo, no windows sampled
future era ────── disc, test ──────────┘
```

| Split | First window (UTC) | Last window (UTC) |
|---|---|---|
| train | 2022-07-01 00:00 | 2024-11-23 01:04 |
| val | 2022-07-12 09:04 | 2024-12-04 10:08 |
| valdisc | 2022-07-23 18:08 | 2024-12-15 19:12 |
| **disc** | **2025-01-30 08:32** | 2026-07-19 01:36 |
| **test** | **2025-02-10 17:36** | 2026-07-30 10:40 |

This is the honest layout for a financial generator — you are asked to generalise forward in
time, not to interpolate inside a shuffled pool — and it has a **price you must state in your
README**: the eras have different volatility.

| Asset | train ann. vol | test ann. vol |
|---|---|---|
| BTCUSDT | 0.498 | **0.429** |
| ETHUSDT | 0.597 | 0.651 |
| BNBUSDT | 0.576 | **0.477** |
| SOLUSDT | 1.039 | **0.733** |
| XRPUSDT | 0.943 | **0.690** |
| DOGEUSDT | 1.107 | **0.800** |
| ADAUSDT | 0.879 | 0.850 |
| LINKUSDT | 0.882 | 0.917 |

Vol falls on six of eight assets. **A generator that reproduces the training law perfectly will
score badly on the volatility rows, and it is right and the metric is right.** Do not "fix" this
by fitting to test.

### 3.4 Microstructure artefacts you must not launder away

`dataset_stats.json` records a **zero-return fraction** per asset: BTC 6.2 %, ETH 4.3 %,
LINK 24.4 %, ADA 22.0 %, BNB 20.9 %. At 30-second bars on the thinner pairs, a fifth to a
quarter of bars have no price change. That is the data, not a bug. Consequences:

- A generator producing continuous-density increments **cannot** match the point mass at zero,
  and will lose A14 (KS) and A16 (QQ) on the thin assets specifically. Report it per asset —
  `metrics_per_asset.csv` exists for exactly this.
- `no_trade_bar_fraction` (LINK 2.3 %, ADA 0.8 %, rest ≤ 0.2 %) counts bars with no trade at
  all, forward-filled. These are a subset of the zero returns.
- **Do not** de-duplicate, jitter, or smooth these away to make a metric look better. If you
  pre-process, the pre-processing is part of your method and goes in `code/README.md`.

### 3.5 Fitting anything but `train` is fabrication

Validation is for hyperparameters. `disc`/`valdisc` are the discriminator's real side. `test` is
never seen until `compute_all_multiasset.py` runs. There is no digest guard on this dataset the
way there is on Heston — the guard is the **variant directory name** plus the tracked
`dataset_stats.json`. Copy that file into `<Method>/losses/dataset_stats.json` so your results
carry their own provenance.

## 4. Generated paths contract

`generated_paths/seed_{i}/generated_paths_6144x128x8.npy`

- shape `(6144, 128, 8)`, dtype `float64`
- **original price scale** — denormalise before saving, never write `[0,1]` outputs
- `S[:, 0, :] == 100.0` exactly
- finite and strictly positive everywhere

**Generate exactly 6 144 paths for the A/B tables, not a round number.** Every real-vs-real
threshold in §7 was measured between real splits of exactly that size, and the volatility
estimator's sampling noise falls like 1/√m — **1.67 pp at m = 512, 1.25 at 1 024, 0.65 at 4 096,
0.42 at 8 192**. A bank of a different size compares a differently-noisy estimate against those
thresholds and the comparison is not valid. The one exception is the conditional-CRPS pool,
pinned to the paper's 8 192 (§8).

If your generator cannot land on `S[:,0,:] == 100.0`, **rescale, never clip**:

```python
Xg = Xg * (100.0 / Xg[:, 0:1, :])   # per-(path, asset) constant multiplier
Xg[:, 0, :] = 100.0                 # remove the float residue from the division
```

Multiplying a whole path by a constant is a shift of the log-price level, so every log-return is
**bit-identical** before and after. Set `"s0_rescaled": true` in `metadata.json`.

`metadata.json` is **tracked in git** and is the audit record for an array that is not. Minimum
fields: `method, seed, shape, dtype, S0, S0_exact, S_min, S_max, gen_time_sec, s0_rescaled`,
plus `data_dir` and `seq_tag` naming the variant. `collect_artifacts.py` must **recompute**
shape/dtype/S0/min/max from the `.npy` rather than trusting what the generator wrote, and must
exit non-zero on a contract violation — it is a gate, not a report. Metrics on a broken array
cost 45 minutes and produce numbers that look completely normal.

Four flags exist **only** for this dataset and all four are mandatory:

| Flag | Why |
|---|---|
| `--data-dir` | the splits live under `variants/<name>/`, not `dataset/TrueDataset/` |
| `--seq-tag` | rewrites the `NxTxd` tag in every filename; the canonical tag is `8192x128x8` and the locked build is `6144x128x8` |
| `--dt` | §3.2 — omit it and every vol row is wrong by 64.6× |
| `--results-dir` | artefacts publish to `results/trueexperiment/`, which `results/<dataset>/<method>` cannot name |

Measured cost: **~8.5 min per seed**, ~42 min for 5 seeds.

### 5.1 Per-asset vs native scoping

Identical to the Heston rule; do not diverge.

| Scope | Metrics | How |
|---|---|---|
| **native d = 8** | **A6-A11, A18, A20, A25** | evaluated once on the full `(N, T, 8)` tensor |
| **per-asset mean** | everything else | on each of the 8 slices, reported as the mean over assets |

**A33-A34 do not exist here.** They score against the dataset's *latent variance* path. A real
market has none, so `DATASET_FILES["TrueDataset"]["v_test"] is None` and the driver skips them.
Your README must **omit the rows**, not render them as `-`: a dash implies the metric was
attempted and failed. `true_rv_*.npy` is a realised-vol *estimate*, not a latent state, and
substituting it would fabricate a ground truth.

**A20 is still the row that matters most.** It is the only metric that tests whether the
cross-asset correlation structure survived generation. Lead with it.

## 6. The real-vs-real reference — shared, do not regenerate

`real_floor/` holds **three held-out REAL splits** — `train`, `val`, `valdisc` — pushed through
the metric pipeline *as if they were generated banks* and scored against `test` with
byte-identical code. Rebuild it only if the dataset variant changes:

```bash
V=dataset/TrueDataset/variants/om_2022-07_N6144; TAG=6144x128x8
R=results/trueexperiment/real_floor
for i in 0 1 2; do mkdir -p $R/generated_paths/seed_$i; done
ln -sf $PWD/$V/true_S_$TAG.npy         $R/generated_paths/seed_0/generated_paths_$TAG.npy
ln -sf $PWD/$V/true_S_val_$TAG.npy     $R/generated_paths/seed_1/generated_paths_$TAG.npy
ln -sf $PWD/$V/true_S_valdisc_$TAG.npy $R/generated_paths/seed_2/generated_paths_$TAG.npy
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 /home/tbasseras/gpu-venv/bin/python \
    metrics/compute_all_multiasset.py --method real_floor --dataset TrueDataset --seeds 3 \
    --data-dir $V --seq-tag $TAG --dt 9.51293759512e-07 --results-dir $R
```

### 6.1 Four things you must not get wrong about it

1. **It is not a target.** It is the score a **perfect memoriser of the training era** achieves
   on the test era. A generator that reaches it has demonstrated it can reproduce the training
   era, which is exactly what a memoriser does.
2. **Beating it is not automatically a red flag.** On Heston, beating the floor means you are
   reusing training data, full stop. Here, part of the floor's distance to `test` is the §3.3
   regime change, and a generator that produces *less* volatility than its training data — for
   any reason, including over-smoothing — can land closer to `test` than the training split
   does. **Beating the floor on a volatility row while NNratio is healthy is a coincidence, not
   a win, and must be described as one.**
3. **`disc` is excluded on purpose.** It is the discriminator's real side. Using it as a fake
   bank drives A18 to 0 by construction and manufactures an unreachable reference.
4. **Three seeds, not five.** Only three non-`test`, non-`disc` real splits exist. The spread in
   that column is a spread over *three real samples*, and it is not the same object as the
   5-seed spread in the method columns. Say so in the caption.

## 7. Hyperparameter selection — the real-vs-real envelope

There is no ground-truth parameter set to recover, so "which `(h, K)` is right" has to be
answered by an admissibility criterion rather than a fit. The one used for SBTS, and the one to
reuse, is pre-registered in [`SBTS/losses/selection_criterion.md`](SBTS/losses/selection_criterion.md).

**The envelope** = the extreme value attained by real market data scored against real market
data over the **20 ordered split pairs**. Zero free parameters. It must be **recomputed per
dataset variant** and never hardcoded. For `om_2022-07_N6144`:

| Statistic | min | median | **ceiling** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vol_err` (%) | 8.72 | 14.96 | **24.59** |
| `corr_err` | 0.0208 | 0.0925 | **0.1843** |

Kurtosis is **not** gated: on 30-second crypto it is dominated by a handful of jump bars and its
split-to-split spread is wider than any difference between candidate hyperparameters.

**Objective, given admissibility:** minimise `|log NNratio|`, where

```
NNratio = median NN-distance(generated -> train) / median NN-distance(val -> train)
```

Tie-break within 0.02: lowest `corr_err`.

### 7.1 Why the objective is NNratio and not goodness of fit

Because goodness of fit selects a memoriser. Measured on this build:

| h | K | vol_err % | corr_err | NNratio | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 3 | **6.06** | — | **0.197** | best fit in the grid; **rejected** |
| 0.06 | 1 | 13.28 ± 1.50 | 0.0914 | 0.911 | admissible |
| **0.07** | **1** | **15.61 ± 2.39** | **0.0951** | **0.923** | **selected** |
| 0.08 | 1 | 18.23 ± 0.60 | 0.1045 | 0.939 | admissible |

`h=0.05, K=3` beats the real-vs-real **minimum** of 8.72 % — impossible for an honest generator
— and its NNratio of 0.197 says why: generated paths sit **five times closer** to the training
set than held-out real data does. Anyone reading only the volatility column picks it.

### 7.2 Screen coarse, confirm at scale

The grid runs at `m_probe = 512`, which is a **coarse screen only**: the vol estimator's noise
at that size is 1.67 pp, and the top four candidates were separated by 0.024 in the objective.
Re-measure anything within ±3.3 pp of the ceiling at `m_probe = 2048` or higher, over **3 seeds**,
before declaring a winner. A screen that hits a grid boundary is a **boundary**, not an optimum;
say so in the README.

## 8. Conditional generation (table C) — REQUIRED, and it is the headline

Everything in §5 scores the **unconditional** law. That is not the question a real market poses.
Table C is the paper's §3.3.1 protocol (Deep-MKV-TS, Table 4), and on this dataset it is the
**primary result**; the A-table is a sanity gate.

```bash
V=dataset/TrueDataset/variants/om_2022-07_N6144
R=results/trueexperiment/<Method>

# 1. the pool: 4 banks of 8 192 paths at the selected hyperparameters
for S in 0 1 2 3; do
  <your generator> --seed $S --m-simu 8192 --out-root $R/crps_banks
done

# 2. score, one file per (config, seed). Both historical baselines are rebuilt
#    inside every run (deterministic given --seed 1234): ~5 min of redundant
#    work that buys a self-contained artefact per file. Worth it.
#
#    RUN BOTH CONFIGS. `paper` is what Table C reports. `perdim` is the
#    robustness check (§8.7) and the renderer's one-line prose claim that the
#    table "stands" is FALSE unless you have actually produced it.
for CFG in paper perdim; do
  [ $CFG = paper ] && WM=paper ST=bank || WM=perdim ST=realtrain
  for S in 0 1 2 3; do
    /home/tbasseras/gpu-venv/bin/python metrics/conditional_crps_multiasset.py \
        --data-dir $V --seq-tag 6144x128x8 --bank-size 8192 --label <Method> \
        --weight-mode $WM --standardize $ST \
        --bank $R/crps_banks/generated_paths/seed_$S/generated_paths_8192x128x8.npy \
        --out $R/losses/crps_configs/${CFG}__seed_$S.json
  done
  # 3. the reference bank: the real training split used directly (§8.4)
  /home/tbasseras/gpu-venv/bin/python metrics/conditional_crps_multiasset.py \
      --data-dir $V --seq-tag 6144x128x8 --bank-size 8192 \
      --weight-mode $WM --standardize $ST \
      --bank $V/true_S_6144x128x8.npy --label real_train_bank \
      --out $R/losses/crps_configs/${CFG}__realbank.json
done
```

**Artefact paths are load-bearing.** `render_readme.py` globs
`losses/crps_configs/paper__seed_*.json` and falls back to the flat legacy
`losses/conditional_crps_seed_*.json` only so an un-rerun checkout still renders. **The legacy
files predate the rv fix of 2026-08-26 (§8.1) and are numerically wrong.** If your method writes
to the legacy path you will silently render against the old convention while `SBTS/` renders
against the new one, and the two columns will not be comparable. Write to `crps_configs/`.

Wall clock, measured: **~150 s per bank** on 6 cores, 3 banks per run (yours + 2 baselines) =
~8 min per (config, seed). 10 runs = ~80 min serial, ~20 min at 7-way parallelism with
`taskset -c` and `OMP_NUM_THREADS=6`. Detach it (`setsid … & disown`), do not background it in
the harness.

**Protocol, fixed — do not tune any of it:**

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| history | first **65 log-prices** (`S_IDX = 64`) | paper §3.3.1 |
| horizon | next **32 returns** (`H = 32`) | paper §3.3.1 |
| neighbours | **K = 256** | paper §3.3.1 (the "252" you may remember is a typo) |
| pool | **8 192** paths, generated *and* both baselines | paper §3.3.1 |
| feature blocks | last 32 returns (w 1.0), 24-pt path shape (w 0.5), rolling vol 5/10/20 (w 2.0), abs+sq ACF at lags 1,2,5,10 (w 1.0) | paper §3.3.1 |
| feature dim | 73 per asset × 8 = **584** | derived |
| baselines | moving-block bootstrap (blocks of 8) **and** session bootstrap | paper §3.3.1 — **both**, not one |
| baseline seed | 1234 | author, `real_data_protocol.py:392` |
| CI | 2 000 bootstrap replicates, 95 %, seed 0 | author, `shadowing.py:1209` |

### 8.1 Three points where the author's code overrides the paper text

Verified against the author's implementation; keep all three:

1. **Block weights are NOT dimension-normalised.** `_standardize_block` returns
   `(weight**0.5) * ((block - mean) / std)` — the √w multiplies *every coordinate*, with no
   division by the block's length. Do not divide by block length. (This has a consequence the
   paper does not discuss; see §8.7.)
2. **Standardisation is frozen on the bank being scored**, not on a shared reference
   (`shadowing.py:303-329`, `reference_blocks=generated_raw`). Each bank gets its own μ, σ. This
   means the two baselines and your generated bank are standardised differently — that is
   intended.
3. **`rv` is realised volatility per asset**, `sqrt(sum_t r²_{t,a})` of shape `(N, A)` — the
   squared increments are summed over **TIME**, giving one scalar per asset per path.

#### 8.1.3 was wrong until 2026-08-26. Read this before you copy anything.

This file previously stated, in this exact slot, that `rv` was a *cross-asset trajectory*
`sqrt(sum_a r²_{t,a})` of shape `(N, H)` — summed over **assets** at each step. That is a
different statistic, and it was implemented that way and shipped.

The author sums over time. `shadowing.py:579-601`: `raw_shadows` is `(B, top_k, h+1, A)` and
`raw_real_continuation` is `(B, h+1, A)`; in both the reduction runs on the **time** axis. At
d = 1 the author's quantity is realised vol over the window, while the wrong one collapses to
`|r_t|`.

The failure mode is worth naming because it is easy to repeat: the docstring **cited the correct
line numbers** while asserting the opposite of what those lines do. The axis *index* was matched;
the axis *meaning* was not. When you port a reduction from someone else's array, write down what
each axis **is** before you write `axis=`.

Consequences, all of which you inherit:

- Every `losses/conditional_crps_seed_*.json` written before 2026-08-26 has a wrong `rv` column.
  `cum_return` and `increment` are unaffected and reproduce bit-identically.
- The SBTS/block-bootstrap rv ratio moved **0.972 → 0.941**, into the paper's 0.808-0.946 band
  rather than just outside it.
- This lands on the single row where the paper's headline sits and where SBTS posts its only
  resolvable result. It was the one cell that could not be left wrong.

### 8.2 One deliberate deviation, and why

**Retrieval is joint across assets** — one 584-dim vector per path. The author's
`real_data_protocol.py:513` hard-raises for `d ≠ 1`, so there is no reference behaviour to copy.
Per-asset retrieval would answer a different question (8 independent univariate forecasts) and
would discard exactly the cross-asset structure this dataset exists to test. If you change this,
you are no longer comparable to `SBTS/` and you must say so in your README.

### 8.3 Read the ratio, not the level

Absolute CRPS is set by the market's intrinsic unpredictability and the units of the data. The
transferable number is the **ratio to the moving-block bootstrap**. Paper Table 4, ES / NQ / YM:

| | cum | incr | rv |
|---|---|---|---|
| SBTS / block bootstrap | 0.998 / 1.088 / 1.087 | 1.026 / 1.067 / **1.121** | **0.808 / 0.916 / 0.946** |

The paper's own SBTS **loses** on cumulative return for two of three indices. Winning there is
not the expected outcome.

> **The YM increment ratio read `1.048` in this file until 2026-08-26. It is `1.121`.**
> `0.297 / 0.265 = 1.1208`. It is the cell where SBTS's increment loss is *worst*, i.e. exactly
> where an optimistic transcription slip lands. **Recompute these from

### 8.4 The window is narrow — measured, not asserted

Reference levels on this build (×1000, 6 144 test queries, `paper` config, post-rv-fix):

| Bank | Bank size | cum | incr | rv |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real **training split** used directly as the bank | 6 144 | 1.257 | 0.335 | 0.970 |
| Session bootstrap (seed 1234) | 8 192 | 1.262 | 0.337 | 0.975 |
| Moving-block bootstrap (seed 1234) | 8 192 | 1.279 | 0.338 | 1.110 |

The real training split — a bank **no generator fitted on that split can beat** — is only
**1.7 %** better than the block bootstrap on `cum` and **0.9 %** on `incr`. Only `rv` (**12.6 %**)
has real room. A method "winning" by 0.5 % on `cum` has demonstrated that the metric is saturated
there, not that it has conditional skill. Say so.

**Note the ordering**: the session bootstrap lands within 0.5 % of the real-training-split bank
on all three targets. Resampling whole real training days is very nearly the best a
train-fitted bank can do on this panel. **That, not the block bootstrap, is the baseline your
method has to survive**, and the paper does not report it as a ratio — so nobody will do it for
you.

### 8.5 Bank size is not a lever

Measured on this build, K = 256 fixed, block bootstrap, **re-run 2026-08-26 with the corrected
`rv`** (the `rv` column here was `0.574-0.584` before the §8.1 fix; those values were wrong):

| M | cum | incr | rv |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 536 | 1.284 | 0.338 | 1.128 |
| 3 072 | 1.284 | 0.338 | 1.088 |
| 6 144 | 1.284 | 0.338 | 1.123 |
| 8 192 | 1.279 | 0.338 | 1.110 |
| 12 288 | 1.279 | 0.337 | 1.090 |

**`cum` and `incr` move 0.4 % over an 8× range — flat.** Averaging 256 neighbours makes the
ensemble spread depend on intrinsic unpredictability, not on retrieval sharpness.

**`rv` is different and the earlier version of this file got it wrong.** It spans 1.088-1.128, a
**3.7 %** range — but **non-monotonically** (1 536 is the worst, 3 072 the best, 12 288
second-best). A real bank-size effect would be monotone. The spread is comparable to the query
CI half-width (±0.026) and is resampling noise from the single bootstrap draw at each M, not a

supporting number is 3.7 % and non-monotone, not 0.4 %.

The lesson is not about bank size. **A stale summary statistic outlives the bug that produced
it.** "0.4 % over an 8× range" was quoted as settled for weeks and was an artefact of the wrong
`rv` axis. When you fix a metric, re-run every sweep that quoted it — grep this file and the
method READMEs for the metric's name before you call the fix done.

### 8.6 Two ± in one table, and they are not the same quantity

Table C prints `mean ± x` in every cell. **The ± means two different things depending on the
row**, and a reader who merges them draws a conclusion that is wrong by a factor of ~25:

| Row | ± is | Typical size (rv) | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bold aggregate **`<Method>`** row | sample **sd across the 4 seeds** | 0.007 | how much does the *generator* wobble? |
| Every per-seed and baseline row | **half-width of the 95 % bootstrap CI over the 6 144 queries** (2 000 replicates, seed 0) | 0.026 | how much would the *average* move on a different draw of test histories? |

Both are stored. The artefacts keep `ci_lo`/`ci_hi`; `_cell()` collapses them to a half-width,
which discards the (small, ~1e-3, sub-precision) asymmetry of a percentile bootstrap. **Your
renderer must print the disambiguating caption.** If you emit two different ± under one column
header with no note, the table is misinformation regardless of the numbers being right.

### 8.7 Run the second retrieval convention, or delete the claim

§8.1.1 says the author does not divide the block weight by the block dimension. That is a
faithful reproduction, and it has a consequence the paper never states: **a block's effective
influence is `weight × dimension`, not `weight`.**

| Block | Declared weight | Dim | Effective mass | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recent returns | 1.0 | 32 | 32 | **46 %** |
| Rolling vol | **2.0** | 9 | 18 | 26 % |
| Path shape | 0.5 | 24 | 12 | 17 % |
| ACF (dependence) | 1.0 | 8 | 8 | 11 % |

The block weighted highest per coordinate carries barely half the mass of the block weighted
lowest. **The declared intent is inverted by dimensionality.** This is not a bug to fix — it is
what the author's code does, so Table C keeps it — but it means the retrieval geometry is an
arbitrary choice, and a ranking that only survives that choice is not a finding.

So run `--weight-mode perdim --standardize realtrain` on the same banks (§8, step 2). Measured
for SBTS on this build:

| Convention | cum ratio | incr ratio | rv ratio | mean NN₁ distance | bank coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `paper` — √w undivided, μ/σ per bank | 0.993 | 0.997 | 0.941 | **26.65** | 98.3 % |
| `perdim` — √(w/dim), μ/σ frozen on real train | 0.994 | 0.998 | 0.948 | **5.71** | 98.5 % |

Two things to take from it:

- **The ratios move by under 1 %, so the SBTS conclusion survives.** That sentence is only
  allowed in your README *if you ran it*. `SBTS/README.md` makes the claim and the artefacts
  backing it are committed at `losses/crps_configs/perdim__*.json`.
- **The geometries are genuinely different** — nearest-neighbour distance drops 4.7× — so the
  agreement is a real robustness result, not two runs of the same thing. If your method's ratios
  *disagree in sign* between the two, the ranking is an artefact of the weighting; report both
  and claim neither.

**`bank coverage`** (`_diag.unique_neighbour_frac`) is the fraction of bank paths retrieved at
least once across all 6 144 × 256 retrievals. It is a degeneracy alarm, not a quality score: at
~98 % retrieval is genuinely conditional; at 5 % every query would be handed nearly the same 256
futures and Table C would be scoring one fixed ensemble, scoring identically for a method with
conditional skill and one with none. With **584 dimensions against an 8 192-path bank**, distance
concentration is a live risk — measure it, do not assume it. `_diag` also carries
`nn1_dist_mean`/`nn1_dist_p95`; `_diag` is excluded from the ×1000 scaling because it holds
distances and fractions, not CRPS.

### 8.8 Count differences the test can resolve, not ratios below 1.000

The headline sentence under Table C is generated. **Do not generate it by counting which side of
1.000 each ratio falls on.** That is what this tree did until 2026-08-26, and it printed:

> SBTS beats the moving-block bootstrap on **3 of the 3 targets**

when two of those three were a **0.009** and a **0.001** gap sitting inside a **±0.025** and
**±0.005** error bar. Three claimed wins, one supportable.

The rule now applied in `render_crps_table`:

```
resolvable(target)  ⟺  |mean_method − mean_baseline|  >  half_method + half_baseline
```

i.e. the two 95 % CIs do not overlap. On SBTS this yields **1 of 3** (realized vol) against the
block bootstrap, and correctly flags the realized-vol *loss* to the session bootstrap as
resolvable too — the gate must not be applied only to the good news.

**This test is deliberately conservative.** Both CIs are computed on the *same* 6 144 queries, so
the comparison is paired; a paired bootstrap on the per-query differences would be tighter and
would likely resolve the increment column as well. It has not been run in this tree. Under-
claiming is the safe direction, and if you run the paired version, say in your README that you
did and that it is a different (sharper) test than the one `SBTS/` used.

### 8.9 This is not the 1M-path bank

If someone mentions a **1 000 000**-path bank, they are thinking of **Morel, Mallat & Bouchaud**
([arXiv:2308.01486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01486)), implemented separately in this repo at
`results/Heston/preprocessing_with_log_returns/*/path_shadowing/path_shadowing_pdf.py` with
`BANK_SIZES = [4096, 16384, 65536, 262144, 1_000_000]`. **Different paper, different protocol.**
Table C is 8 192, from Deep-MKV-TS §3.3.1. Do not mix them.

## 9. Memorisation diagnostic — required, and here it is the ONLY guard

On Heston, a metric landing below the floor exposes memorisation. **Here it does not**, for the
reason in §6.1.2. NNratio is therefore not a supplementary diagnostic on this dataset — it is
the sole defence.

```
NNratio = median NN-distance(generated -> train) / median NN-distance(val -> train)
```

Write `losses/memorisation.json` and report the number in the README **whatever it says**.

- **NNratio ≪ 1** → generated paths hug the training set more tightly than genuinely held-out
  real data does. Memorisation. The value 0.197 in §7.1 is what this looks like.
- **NNratio ≈ 1** → the generator is as far from train as fresh real data is. Healthy.
- **NNratio ≫ 1** → the generator is off-manifold; check the A-table for a collapse.
- **A near-1.0 ratio is not a pass on its own.** It is necessary, not sufficient — a method can
  interpolate between two training paths and land at ratio 1 while producing nothing new.
  Report it alongside A18: a discriminator that cannot separate real from generated *and* a
  healthy NNratio is the pair you want.

The admissible band measured on this build is **0.932-1.000**. That is not a chosen tolerance:
its two endpoints are simply what the real splits score against each other — `test`/`val` = 0.932
and `val`/`val` = 1.000. It is the real-vs-real range, with the same zero free parameters as the
§6 envelope.

### 9.1 The denominator is `val`, not `test` — do not copy the Heston file blindly

`results/HestonMultiAsset/SBTS/code/measure_memorisation.py` puts the **test** split in the
denominator, and on Heston that is exactly right: every split there is an i.i.d. draw from one
known SDE, so `test` means "genuine data from the same law, unseen" and nothing else.

TrueDataset is not that. `split_mode` is `holdout-era` (§3.3): train / val / valdisc come from
2022-07 → 2024-12, disc / test from 2025-02 → 2026-07, and annualised vol **falls** between the
eras on six of the eight assets (BTC 0.498 → 0.429, SOL 1.039 → 0.733, DOGE 1.107 → 0.800).

Measured on this build:

| denominator | median NN → train | ratio to `val` |
|---|---:|---:|
| `val` (same era as train) | 0.018943 | 1.000 |
| `test` (later era) | 0.017647 | **0.932** |

**Read the direction, it is the opposite of the intuition.** The test era is not *further* from
train for being a different regime — it is **closer**. Nothing about it resembles the training
data more; its returns are simply smaller, so every distance in the space contracts with them.
The test denominator measures a volatility level, not a similarity.

Using it anyway biases the diagnostic the dangerous way. A smaller denominator makes every ratio
**larger** — the same SBTS banks score **1.081** against `val` and **1.161** against `test`.
Memorisation is the *low*-ratio failure, so a test denominator pushes a memorising generator
**up**, toward the healthy-looking end, and does it silently.

So: **`val` is the denominator** — same era as train, so it answers the actual question, and it
is the same denominator the (h, K) sweep was scored against, which is what makes the number in
`memorisation.json` comparable to the sweep. Report the test-era ratio too, as
`nn_ratio_vs_test_era`; the gap between the two is a direct readout of the regime change, and
hiding it would be its own defect.

- Copy `dataset_stats.json` into `<Method>/losses/` so the results carry their own provenance
  even if the variant directory is later rebuilt.

## 13. Traps already hit on this dataset

**13.1 The metrics driver had no `--data-dir`, `--seq-tag` or `--results-dir`.** All three were
added for this dataset. If you are reading an older copy of `compute_all_multiasset.py`, it will
silently look in `dataset/TrueDataset/` for `*_8192x128x8.npy` and write to
`results/TrueDataset/<Method>/`. Both are wrong, and neither errors loudly.

**13.2 A dynamically-imported generator module must be registered in `sys.modules`.**
`generate_bank_true.py` loads its library through `importlib`. Generation fans out over
`multiprocessing`, and fork+pickle pickles the worker *by qualified name*; an unregistered module
makes that name resolve to a different object in the child and the run dies with
`it's not the same object as sbts_generate_true._worker` — **after** the fan-out has launched.
Insert into `sys.modules` before `exec_module`. Smoke-test on a 32-path bank before committing an
hour of compute.

**13.3 The wrong interpreter costs a full run.** `~/.cc-venv` has no torch;
`compute_all_multiasset.py` dies on import after you have waited for the queue.
**Numba/SBTS → `/home/tbasseras/sbts-venv/bin/python`. Anything importing torch →
`/home/tbasseras/gpu-venv/bin/python`. pandas/dataset work → `/home/tbasseras/.cc-venv/bin/python`.**

**13.4 The session bootstrap is not optional.** The paper specifies **two** historical
baselines and Table 4 reports both for all three indices. An implementation that ships only the
moving-block bootstrap is incomplete, however tempting it is to read the author's code as
authority — on this point the paper text is the spec.

**13.5 A hyperparameter screen that found no separation was nearly reported as if it had.**
Twelve grid rows at `m_probe = 512`, nine admissible, top four separated by 0.024 in an objective
whose measurement noise at that sample size is larger. That is *no result*, not a winner. §7.2.

**13.6 Long chains must be detached.** Multi-hour jobs run under
`setsid nohup <script> > <log> 2>&1 < /dev/null & disown`. A backgrounded job the harness owns
gets reaped when the harness moves on; one such mistake cost 50 minutes of idle GPU. Note that
`$!` after `setsid` is the wrong PID — read the log or `pgrep -af`.

**13.7 `dt` and a generator's internal `DT` are different objects.** §3.2. SBTS's module-level
`DT = 1/252` does not mean the data is daily and changing it changes nothing.

**13.8 A reduction axis silently changed the meaning of a metric, and the docstring vouched for
it.** The `rv` target summed squared increments over the **asset** axis instead of the **time**
axis, turning realised volatility over the horizon into a cross-sectional dispersion at each step.
It ran for weeks, produced plausible numbers, and the docstring above it cited the correct
`shadowing.py` line numbers while asserting the opposite of what those lines do. Full post-mortem
in §8.1.3. Three generalisations, all of which cost time here:

- **At `d = 1` the two axes coincide**, so no d = 1 test can catch this. Any reduction you port
  from a univariate reference needs a `d > 1` test whose expected value you derived by hand.
- **A citation is not a check.** Line numbers in a comment prove someone once looked; they prove
  nothing about the code beneath them. When you port a formula, paste the reference's array shapes
  into the comment — `(B, top_k, h+1, A)` — because a shape comment is falsifiable and a prose
  claim is not.
- **Fixes must be surgical and proven surgical.** After the patch, `cum` and `incr` had to
  reproduce bit-identically and only `rv` was allowed to move. It did: cum 1.2695 → 1.270 (print
  precision only), rv 0.5648 → 1.045. If a "targeted" fix moves a number it should not have
  touched, you fixed the wrong thing.

**13.9 A summary statistic outlived the bug it was computed on.** §8.5 quoted "0.4 % across an
8× bank-size range" as evidence that bank size is not a lever. That number was computed on the
broken `rv` of §13.8. Re-run after the fix it is **3.7 % and non-monotone** — a weaker claim,
still supporting the same conclusion, but the original was not evidence of anything.

> **When you fix a metric, re-run every sweep, table and sentence that quoted it.** Grep the
> guideline and every README for the metric's name before you close the fix. A bug fix that
> leaves stale derived numbers in prose has converted one wrong number into several, and the
> survivors are now harder to find because the code is correct.

**13.10 Two of the scariest bugs this session were in the diagnostic, not the code.** A
`pgrep -af ... | sed 's/--data-dir.*--bank //'` was read as proof that the running jobs had been
launched without `--data-dir`/`--seq-tag`; the flags were there, the `sed` was eating them. And
`ls` of an artefact directory returned empty because the shell was in `$HOME` rather than the
repo root. Both nearly triggered an unnecessary relaunch of hours of compute.

- **Prefix every command in this repo with `cd /home/tbasseras/benchmark &&`.** The harness's
  working directory is not stable across calls.
- **Before declaring a running job broken, verify against the launcher on disk**
  (`grep -n 'data-dir\|seq-tag' /tmp/launch_crps.sh`), never against a filtered view of `ps`.
  Killing a correct 3-hour run on the strength of your own `sed` is the most expensive class of
  mistake available here.
